Understanding poverty and homelessness in our community

A short survey to help us understand how poverty and homelessness affect people locally, and what kind of support actually helps. It takes about 3 minutes.

What do we mean by "poverty" and "homelessness"?

Poverty means not having enough income or resources to meet basic needs — stable housing, enough food, healthcare, and other essentials most people take for granted. It doesn't always look like what people expect; someone in poverty may still be working, still have a home, and still look "fine" from the outside.

Homelessness is broader than sleeping on the street. It includes people staying in cars, on friends' couches, in emergency motels, or moving between temporary places with nowhere stable to call home.

Why this matters

Poverty and homelessness are often invisible in day-to-day life, which makes them easy to misunderstand or overlook. Surveys like this one help build a clearer, more accurate picture of what's actually happening in a community — not assumptions, but what people are genuinely experiencing and what kind of help would make a real difference.

It can happen to anyone

Job loss, illness, a relationship breakdown, or a single unexpected bill can be enough to tip a stable household into crisis.

It's often temporary

Most people who experience poverty or homelessness move in and out of it — it isn't usually a fixed, permanent state.

Children are affected too

Housing instability and poverty affect school attendance, concentration, and long-term wellbeing for kids in the household.

Support reduces harm

Access to food banks, emergency housing, and community services measurably shortens how long a crisis lasts for a family.

Common misconceptions

MythPeople experiencing poverty aren't working.
RealityMany people in poverty are employed — wages simply don't stretch far enough to cover rent, food, and bills.
MythHomelessness is a choice.
RealityMost people experiencing homelessness are there because of circumstances outside their control — housing costs, illness, or family breakdown.
MythIt's a small, rare problem.
RealityHousing insecurity touches far more households than is visible — many people experiencing it are never seen on the street.
